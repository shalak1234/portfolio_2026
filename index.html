Overview

Pomodoro was created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s for studying. You set a 25-minute timer and start. It worked well in a university setup, because Francesco had a pre-defined structure for tasks: A chapter, a problem set, a topic.

The Problem

When we try to use pomodoro in product work, it’s not that clear. ‘Work on the dashboard redesign’ could mean feedback review, flow changes, UI polish, or stakeholder sync.

That choice creates friction. You either hesitate or pick something easy to do. Pomodoro defines time but it doesn’t define intention.

How Can We Solve This?

To bring back that structure back into product work, people need an easy way to decide what they want to get done when they start their session.

If we make it very easy for people to quickly pick what they want to do right before starting their pomodoro, we can make pomodoro a lot more useful for product work.

Intervention

Choose a pomodoro → Choose tasks to finish → Start the session

Having your tasks right there when you start a timer makes it really easy to do the most important task: Deciding what to do.

So I built a PWA on Lovable.

Check it out

Long-Term Benefits

This small change of habit when doing pomodoro reinforces your relationship between effort and intention. Do this a couple of times, it makes you aware of how much you can get done in just 25 minutes.

It helps you differentiate between high-impact work, and low-impact work, and helps your prioritise how you can do more of things that matter more to you.

Exploration

While building this, I explored and idea of an interaction which mimics turning a physical knob to set a timer. I could not include this in the final product, because I wanted the pomodoros to be in a pair of 2, but if was fun to build and show friends!
